# Governance and authority

Managing attorney is accountable for legal judgment, confidentiality, permitted use, policy changes and release approval. Technical operator (unassigned) implements, monitors and restores systems; no independent legal authority. Staff roles may review or propose within assigned matters; exact delegation must be approved. COO agent coordinates only; specialists and reviewer agents produce drafts and exceptions, not final legal decisions.

| Activity | Default |
| --- | --- |
| Synthetic test processing | Permitted within development scope |
| Live data read | Disabled until specific scope/terms/owner approved |
| Analyze authorized records and prepare drafts | Allowed only within approved live pilot policy |
| Calendar, Clio or Dropbox mutation; external email | Denied unless explicitly enabled and exact action approved |
| Conflict clearance, engagement, final legal advice, court filing | Attorney-controlled outside autonomous execution |
| Destruction of originals or held evidence | Prohibited by default |
| Infrastructure, IAM, billing, release or data retention change | Separate explicit owner approval |

Approval record: reviewer identity and role, matter, proposal ID, canonical action digest, source and policy versions, timestamp, expiry, decision, reason and revocation status. Approval cannot be inferred from silence, an email instruction, an AI recommendation or broad access. Separate deployment authorization from legal-workflow authorization.

Matter access and retention policy must be approved before live use. Preserve originals and chain of custody; flag legal holds. Current law, local rules, deadlines, legal research rights and citations require attorney verification. Escalate uncertain identity, deadlines, unsupported claims, privilege risk, conflicting evidence, outages and suspected incidents. Define named owners, escalation contacts and substitute reviewer before pilot. The baseline is policy intent pending attorney adoption and technical enforcement.
